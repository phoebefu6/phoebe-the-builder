1. The split reports one draw of an ORIGIN, and the origin moves the number more than the model
====================================================================================================
  model               true 12-step RMSE  mean split RMSE  sd across draws  p(split < true/2)
  naive                           9.266            8.735            2.392              0.013
  seasonal naive                  5.895            5.699            1.064              0.003
  drift                           9.102            8.643            2.211              0.007
  mean                           15.025           14.944            1.216              0.000
  trend+season                    4.023            3.915            0.802              0.000
  flexible (deg 6)                6.978            6.225            2.545              0.080

  the two closest models in TRUTH are 'drift' (9.102) and 'naive' (9.266) - a gap of 0.164
  a single split's spread for the better one is sd 2.211 = 13.50x that gap
  so one split ranks them BACKWARDS 0.317 of the time, on 300 independent series
  the pair a project actually chooses between - 'trend+season' (4.023) vs 'seasonal naive' (5.895), a gap of 1.872 - is
  ranked backwards 0.030 of the time

  DERIVED HERE: the split does not merely add noise to a comparison, it TILTS it. Its
  mean sits below the truth, and the shortfall grows with the model's own error spread:
  model               relative sd  split understates truth by
  mean                      0.081                        0.5%
  seasonal naive            0.181                        3.3%
  trend+season              0.199                        2.7%
  drift                     0.243                        5.0%
  naive                     0.258                        5.7%
  flexible (deg 6)          0.365                       10.8%
  correlation across the six models: 0.972
  consequence: 'seasonal naive' (5.895) vs 'flexible (deg 6)' (6.978) is
  ranked backwards 0.527 of the time - WORSE than a coin flip, so a
  single split is not a weak test of that comparison, it is a wrong one.

  within ONE series, moving the origin over 60 choices gives RMSE 3.043 to 5.747 (sd 0.674)
  the honest number is 4.023; the best origin available to report is 75.7% of it

3. Random k-fold on a lag table: the leak is a function of rho, and the RANKING survives it
====================================================================================================
  a stationary seasonal series (trend removed, so 'fit here, score later' is not also a
  test of extrapolation), a p=6 lag model, k=5. The honest column fits the whole pool and
  scores a genuinely later stretch of the same series.

   AR(1) rho   random k-fold   blocked+embargo   honest future   random optimism
        0.00           4.611             4.591           4.600              0.2%
        0.30           4.303             4.287           4.272              0.7%
        0.60           3.611             3.627           3.605              0.2%
        0.90           2.104             2.118           2.107             -0.2%

  NEGATIVE RESULT: at this model size the shuffle is nearly harmless, and the reason is
  that the ROW ALREADY CONTAINS its neighbours - a lag table's features are the very
  values the shuffle is accused of leaking, so a 6-parameter model has nothing extra to
  do with them. The warning as usually stated ('never shuffle a time series') names the
  wrong culprit.

    lags p   shuffled CV   honest future    optimism
         3         3.821           3.789        0.8%
        12         3.558           3.493        1.8%
        40         3.839           3.633        5.7%
        80         5.716           4.388       30.3%
  the shuffle's damage is not in the correlation of the SERIES, it is in the CAPACITY of
  the model: shuffling buys an over-parameterised fit neighbours to interpolate between,
  and the optimism grows with p while rho is held fixed.

4. Rolling-origin folds are not independent, so the published standard error is not one
====================================================================================================
  k=20 step= 1 (overlap 11/12): reported SE 0.1244  true SE 0.5605  -> k_eff 1.0 of 20 (5%)
  k=20 step= 6 (overlap 6/12): reported SE 0.1782  true SE 0.2546  -> k_eff 9.8 of 20 (49%)
  k=20 step=12 (overlap 0/12): reported SE 0.1813  true SE 0.2338  -> k_eff 12.0 of 20 (60%)
  k= 8 step=12 (overlap 0/12): reported SE 0.2809  true SE 0.2954  -> k_eff 7.2 of 8 (90%)

  the overlap is not the only cause - every origin also shares the SAME training data and
  the same realised series, which is why even non-overlapping windows (step=12) do not
  recover k independent folds.

6. After a break, expanding vs sliding is a real choice - and the backtest cannot make it
====================================================================================================
  world                          expanding      sliding 36      sliding 72          best
  no break                           3.872           4.252           3.997     expanding
  level break +25                    4.465           4.313           9.753    sliding 36
  trend break 0.15->0.60            16.323           4.321           4.263    sliding 72


  Can the backtest itself pick the right one? Score both protocols on the origins BEFORE
  the final one, take the winner, and check it against what actually wins on the future:
  no break                 picks the future's winner 0.455 of the time, mean regret +0.148 RMSE
                             it picks   {'expanding': 148, 'sliding 36': 6, 'sliding 72': 46}
                             truth is   {'expanding': 112, 'sliding 36': 45, 'sliding 72': 43}
  level break +25          picks the future's winner 0.075 of the time, mean regret +5.235 RMSE
                             it picks   {'expanding': 10, 'sliding 36': 17, 'sliding 72': 173}
                             truth is   {'expanding': 77, 'sliding 36': 123}
  trend break 0.15->0.60   picks the future's winner 0.540 of the time, mean regret +0.258 RMSE
                             it picks   {'sliding 36': 200}
                             truth is   {'sliding 36': 108, 'sliding 72': 92}

  NEGATIVE RESULT, and it reverses what this section looked like one experiment ago: the
  protocol comparison is PAIRED on the same origins, which removes the origin noise that
  wrecks section 1 - and it is still wrong 0.925 of the time after a level break, at a
  regret larger than the error itself. The reason is not noise. The backtest's origins
  and the DEPLOYMENT origin stand at different distances from the break, so a window that
  straddles the break during the backtest no longer straddles it when you ship.

  The mechanism is testable: move the break far enough back that every backtest origin AND
  the deployment origin sit entirely after it, and the same code should recover.
  break 58 before deployment     accuracy 0.050   mean regret +5.213
  break 158 before deployment    accuracy 0.625   mean regret +0.125
  so the protocol choice is answerable, but only once the break is old enough that the
  backtest and the deployment are asking the same question - which is roughly when the
  choice has stopped mattering.

7. MAPE does not rank forecasts by accuracy - it rewards forecasting low
====================================================================================================
  a lower-level, noisier series (level 40, sigma 12, i.e. a CV around 30%) - the regime every demand and count series lives in

  shift added to the forecast       MAPE %      RMSE
                          -14.0    29.407    18.338
                           -6.0    22.474    13.361  <- min MAPE
                           +0.0    23.713    11.886  <- min RMSE
                           +6.0    27.339    13.021

  MAPE is minimised at a shift of -6.0, RMSE at +0.0
  deliberately forecasting 6 units LOW (15% of the
  level) improves MAPE from 23.713 to 22.474 (-5.2%) while RMSE gets +12.4% worse
  a leaderboard scored on MAPE is partly a leaderboard of downward bias, and the size of
  the reward is a property of the SERIES (its coefficient of variation), not of the model.
