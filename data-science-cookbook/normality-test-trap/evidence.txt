================================================================================================
NORMALITY-TEST TRAP: what a Shapiro-Wilk gate actually does to a t-test
================================================================================================

Every population below is standardised to mean 0, sd 1. The null tested is 'the
population mean is 0'. It is true in every single cell. So every rejection printed
is a false positive, and a correct 0.05 test should show 0.05 everywhere.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   The two tables run in OPPOSITE directions. Shapiro's certainty grows with n;
   the t-test's error shrinks with n, because the CLT is repairing exactly the
   thing Shapiro is getting better at detecting.

   A right-skewed population makes the t-test reject DOWNWARD far more often than
   upward, because a sample that happens to miss the long right tail has both a low
   mean and a low variance - and the low variance shrinks the standard error that
   the low mean is divided by. The two errors do not cancel, they compound. A cell
   can sit at 0.050 two-sided and still be a one-directional test.

7. SHAPIRO-WILK SCORED AS WHAT PEOPLE USE IT FOR: A GATE ON THE T-TEST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Positive  = the gate fires (Shapiro rejects the majority of samples at this n)
  Condition = the t-test's real error rate is outside [0.045, 0.055]

  grid cells                 63
  cells where t IS broken    16
  sensitivity (catches it)   0.812
  specificity (stays quiet)  0.383
  false alarms               29 cells
  misses                     3 cells
  cells too noisy to judge   0

   Specificity is the number that matters. The gate's job, as people use it, is to
   stop you using a t-test that would mislead you. A gate that fires on cell after
   cell where the t-test is already correct is not being cautious - it is routing
   you to a substitute test, and section 6 is what the substitute costs.
