0b. A known-truth cell, so every rate below can be read against the harness's noise
===================================================================================
  Student's t on balanced, equal-variance, normal data is EXACT: its Type I error is 0.05
  by construction, not by approximation. So whatever this harness measures there is pure
  Monte-Carlo noise, and it is the resolution limit for every other cell.
  10 independent runs: min 0.0478, max 0.0514, mean 0.0502
  So a rate inside 0.95x-1.04x nominal is indistinguishable from exact here.
  Read every 'INFLATED' verdict below against that: the Wilson interval is a statistical
  test, and at 40,000 reps it will flag a 1.05x departure that means nothing in practice.

2. Negative result: unequal variance is NOT the diagnostic, imbalance is
========================================================================
  balanced n=20/20, sd_ratio=1: Student 0.0524  -> 1.05x nominal
  balanced n=20/20, sd_ratio=2: Student 0.0552  -> 1.10x nominal
  balanced n=20/20, sd_ratio=3: Student 0.0520  -> within the noise floor
  A 3x variance gap with equal group sizes costs Student's test almost nothing. The rule of
  thumb 'check for equal variances' is testing the wrong thing on its own.
  And the mechanism is visible in one number: at n=50/10 with sd 1/3, Student's test spends
  df=58 while Welch's honest df is 9.24.

4. Negative result: Welch does not fix non-normality, and under skew it is the worse test
=========================================================================================
  distribution design         Student     Welch
  normal       n=20/20          0.0478    0.0475
  normal       n=50/10          0.0495    0.0508
  normal       n=10/50          0.0509    0.0515
  uniform      n=20/20          0.0513    0.0511
  uniform      n=50/10          0.0478    0.0543
  uniform      n=10/50          0.0501    0.0565
  t3           n=20/20          0.0466    0.0459
  t3           n=50/10          0.0538    0.0428
  t3           n=10/50          0.0526    0.0415
  lognormal    n=20/20          0.0388    0.0355
  lognormal    n=50/10          0.0508    0.0951
  lognormal    n=10/50          0.0483    0.0936

  Under a lognormal with unequal n, the ranking REVERSES: Student's holds at ~0.049 and
  Welch runs at ~0.094. Separate variance estimates are the problem - for a skewed variable
  the sample mean and sample variance are correlated, so the n=10 group's variance is small
  exactly when its mean is low, and the statistic's tail is no longer a t.

  How much n does the CLT need to repair it?
    n=50/10     Student 0.0503   Welch 0.0952  (INFLATED (1.90x nominal))
    n=150/30     Student 0.0452   Welch 0.0790  (INFLATED (1.58x nominal))
    n=500/100    Student 0.0481   Welch 0.0640  (INFLATED (1.28x nominal))
    n=2500/500    Student 0.0478   Welch 0.0535  (INFLATED (1.07x nominal))
    Welch needs 3,000 observations to come back to nominal on a skew this ordinary.

5. What to do on Monday
=======================
  * Default to Welch. Over 15 normal-data cells it stayed within [0.0483, 0.0532]; 1 of them departed by more than the harness's
    own noise floor. The premium on balanced designs is at most 0.0051 of power.
  * The thing to check is not 'are the variances equal', it is 'are the groups the same
    size'. Balanced plus a 3x variance gap left Student's at 0.0520; unbalanced plus the same gap is 0.2940 (5.9x nominal).
  * Never read a t statistic off the normal curve. It was above nominal in all 15 cells, up to 0.0811 (1.6x), and the right quantile is free.
  * Welch is not a normality fix. On a lognormal with n=50/10 it ran at 0.0951 while Student's
    held at 0.0508. Bootstrap or transform there; do not assume
    the robust-sounding test is robust to the thing it was not built for.
