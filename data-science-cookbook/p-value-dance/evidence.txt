====================================================================================================
A P-VALUE IS A RANDOM VARIABLE, AND THE SPREAD OF THAT VARIABLE IS THE RESULT
====================================================================================================

Test: Student's two-sample t, equal n, equal variance, normal data, alpha = 0.05.
That configuration is not a convenience - Day 174 verified it is the one cell where Student's
t is EXACT. So nothing below can be blamed on a violated assumption or a wrong test.
Grid: 20 designs x 40,000 replicates. Seed = index into DESIGNS.

  NEGATIVE RESULT, and it reverses the folk version of this story: the spread is WIDEST where
  power is highest and NARROWEST under the null. 'The p-value bounces around over orders of
  magnitude' is true of every design here including the ones nobody would worry about. Raw
  spread is not the hazard; it is not even correlated with the hazard in the right direction.

  The decision-relevant statistic is whether the central 90% STRADDLES 0.05: true on 11 of 15 designs with a real effect.
  Highest-power design that still straddles:
    d=0.5, n=100, power 0.9411 - p95 is 0.0599, so roughly one
    identical rerun in twenty reports 'no significant difference'.

  SECOND NEGATIVE RESULT, sharper: for d > 0 that straddle predicate is EXACTLY 'power < 0.95'
  - p95 < alpha if and only if P(p < alpha) > 0.95. The decision-relevant dance is not an extra
  hazard sitting on top of low power. It IS low power, restated in a more alarming vocabulary.
  Anything this build says about the dance that is not about power is in section 2 or 3.

  For scale, n per group needed for 80% power:
    d = 0.2: n = 394 per group
    d = 0.5: n = 64 per group
    d = 0.8: n = 26 per group

3. REPLICATION - you observed p = 0.05 once. What does an identical rerun say?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Studies are drawn until one lands in p in [0.045, 0.055]; that study is then rerun from the
SAME true effect. 200,000 draws per design.

d=0.8, n=20       6236   7.67e-05    0.0150     0.400     0.6892   0.6934     False

  The replication significance rate differs from the plain unconditional power on 0 of 4 designs.

  THIRD NEGATIVE RESULT: conditioning on 'you observed p = 0.05' buys nothing. With the true
  effect held fixed, replicate p-values are independent, so the rerun's chance of being
  significant is just the study's power - the same number it was before anyone looked. The
  alarming 'a replication of p=0.05 could land anywhere from 0.0001 to 0.44' is the ordinary
  sampling distribution of p at that power, not something the observed 0.05 told you.
  What people actually mean by the dance is that they do NOT know the true effect, and a
  single p = 0.05 is weak evidence about it - which is section 4, not this one.

    0.2/50: 74.5% of reruns come back above p = 0.10.
    0.5/20: 53.3% of reruns come back above p = 0.10.
    0.5/50: 19.9% of reruns come back above p = 0.10.
    0.8/20: 20.6% of reruns come back above p = 0.10.

4. WHAT A SIGNIFICANT RESULT IS WORTH - simulated from a mixture, then checked
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

200,000 studies per row. A share `prior` of them have a real effect; the rest are
exactly null. Among the ones that come out significant, how many were null all along?
The closed form is a CHECK on the simulation, not a substitute for it.

  Worst row: prior 10%, d = 0.2, n = 20 -> 82.6% of everything that clears p < 0.05 is null.
  alpha is the false-positive rate among TRUE NULLS. It is not the error rate of the claims
  you publish, and the gap between the two is set by power and by the prior - neither of which
  appears anywhere in the p-value.

====================================================================================================
WHAT TO TAKE AWAY
====================================================================================================

1. The p-value really is a random variable, and its spread really is enormous - but the spread
   is LARGEST at high power, where it changes no decision. Raw spread is not the finding.
2. The decision-relevant version of the dance is exactly 'power < 0.95'. It carries no
   information beyond the power calculation you should already have run.
3. What power does NOT tell you is the winner's curse. At low power the significant results
   are inflated up to 5.9x and up to 12.6% of them point the wrong way.
4. Conditioning on the observed p = 0.05 tells you nothing extra about the rerun.
5. With a realistic prior, up to 83% of significant findings here are null - and alpha never sees it.
