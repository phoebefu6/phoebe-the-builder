ARE is the asymptotic relative efficiency of Mann-Whitney to the t-test UNDER A LOCATION
SHIFT: 12 * sigma^2 * (integral f^2)^2, and sigma = 1 here by construction. Above 1 means
Mann-Whitney needs FEWER observations for the same power. Three of the six have closed
forms - normal 3/pi, uniform exactly 1, centred exponential exactly 3 - and they all land.

Hand-rolled uncorrected Mann-Whitney vs SciPy on TIE-FREE data: max |dp| = 3.469e-17
So in section 5 the only thing separating the two columns is the tie term.

================================================================================================
1. THE HEADLINE - two defensible tests, one dataset, opposite signs
================================================================================================

The design, in closed form - no simulation involved in any of these four numbers:

  control    N(0, 0.5^2)
  treated    0.7 * N(-1.0, 0.5^2)  +  0.3 * N(5.0, 0.5^2)

  true mean difference   mu_B - mu_A  = +0.8000   -> the t-test's target says treated is HIGHER
  true P(treated > control)           = 0.35505   -> Mann-Whitney's target says treated is LOWER
  treated SD                          = 2.7946
  population Cohen's d                = 0.3985

Most treated cases end up slightly worse; a minority end up a lot better. Both tests are
right. They are answering different questions, and the questions have opposite answers.

Contradiction rate 1.0000 at n = 500 per group (99% CI [0.9998, 1.0000]).
Read that as: on this design, at that sample size, you can run both tests, have both clear
p < 0.05, and get opposite conclusions about the direction of the effect.

The uncomfortable part is the SHAPE of that column. The contradiction rate RISES
monotonically with n - more data does not resolve it, more data guarantees it. At the
small sample sizes where an analyst might expect trouble the two tests simply fail to
disagree, because neither has the power to say anything; the contradiction arrives
exactly when both tests become trustworthy.
  monotone in n: True

Side finding at n = 10: the t-test fires DOWNWARD 0.0303 of the
time against 0.0564 upward, so 34.9%
of its significant results at that size point the wrong way about its OWN target. A sample
from this mixture that happens to miss the high component has both a low mean and a low
variance, and the low variance shrinks the standard error the low mean is divided by - the
same mechanism Day 175 measured as a tail split under skew.

So as a POWER decision the folk advice is sound - the rank test gives up a few per cent
on normal data and wins outright everywhere else. The cost of the swap is not power.

The measured column sits BELOW the predicted one almost everywhere, and the gap grows
with the prediction. That is expected rather than a discrepancy: ARE is a limit as the
effect goes to zero and n goes to infinity, while these are finite-n readings at
d = 0.35 and n = 65, where the high-efficiency shapes have
already pushed Mann-Whitney's power into the flat top of the curve and interpolation
has little room left to work in. The two columns are checked for agreement in ORDER,
not in value - Spearman across the six shapes is asserted in the test suite.

Negative result from the gate: the comparison is NOT available everywhere. Welch is
measurably conservative on the contaminated population at small n, which disqualifies
three cells - and they are the cells where Mann-Whitney's advantage looks largest, so
dropping the gate would have inflated exactly the rows that most flatter the rank test.

================================================================================================
3. THE COST NOBODY MENTIONS - unequal spread, where the MW null is EXACTLY true
================================================================================================

Both groups are symmetric with the same centre, and only the SD differs. For symmetric
populations that makes P(B > A) EXACTLY 0.5, so Mann-Whitney's own null hypothesis is true -
and the mean null is true as well. Any departure from 5% is not a violated hypothesis. It is
the test's variance formula, which assumes the two distributions are IDENTICAL rather than
merely balanced. Welch's t-test is the comparator because Day 176 established it as the one
procedure that held its rate on every design in that grid.

The direction is set by WHICH group is the wide one. Wide group small -> the rank test
fires too often; wide group large -> it goes quiet and the power is simply gone. Both are
failures, and only one of them looks like a failure.

Unequal n is NOT the precondition, which is the part that separates this from Day 176's
Student's-vs-Welch result. Mann-Whitney breaks on 5 of 12 BALANCED cells as well as 16 of 24 unbalanced ones.
  Worst balanced cell: uniform SD ratio 4.0 at n = 30/30 -> 0.0794 (1.59x nominal)
What unequal n changes is the SIZE and the sign. Balanced designs are inflated by about
half again; put the wide group in the small arm and it is three and a half times nominal,
put it in the large arm and the test all but stops firing.

Welch is not perfect here either, and saying otherwise would be the easy version of this
table. It is measurably conservative on 7 cells, all of them the
contaminated population, where one observation in twenty comes from a five-times-wider
normal. But the two failures are not the same size:
  Welch's worst departure       0.0416  =  1.20x off nominal
  Mann-Whitney's worst departure 0.0036  =  13.9x off nominal
A test that is a fifth conservative and a test that is 14 times too quiet are both wrong
and they are not comparably wrong.

================================================================================================
4. TIES - a rank test on a rating scale
================================================================================================

Ties only ever SHRINK Var(U). The no-ties formula therefore divides by a standard error
that is too large, and the uncorrected test goes quiet. SciPy applies the correction; a lot
of hand-rolled rank tests and spreadsheet macros do not.

n = 40 per group, 40,000 replicates a cell, power measured at d = 0.5.

 levels  SD corr/uncorr  null corrected             ?  null uncorrected             ?  power corr  power uncorr
      2          0.8606          0.0562            ok            0.0172  CONSERVATIVE      0.4263        0.2542
      3          0.9384          0.0499            ok            0.0357  CONSERVATIVE      0.4946        0.4299
      5          0.9768          0.0488            ok            0.0434            ok      0.5410        0.5177
      7          0.9874          0.0503            ok            0.0473            ok      0.5563        0.5430

On a 2-point scale the tie correction shrinks the standard error to 0.8606 of the no-ties value,
and dropping it takes the test from 0.0562 to 0.0172 under a true null - 3.3x too quiet - costing 0.1720 of power.

Negative result, and it is a negative result about this study rather than about the test:
the CORRECTED column is flagged on 0 of 4 scales. The
2-point cell is the highest in the table at 0.0562 and it is above
nominal, but its 99% Wilson interval still touches the band, so this build does not have
the resolution to call it broken and does not claim to. The claim that survives is the
one-sided one: applying the tie correction is necessary, and on the evidence here it is
also sufficient.

================================================================================================
SUMMARY
================================================================================================

1. Mann-Whitney and the t-test answer different questions. On a design whose true mean
   difference is +0.80 and whose true P(B>A) is 0.3551, both tests clear p < 0.05 and
   point OPPOSITE ways 100.0% of the time at n = 500 per group.
2. As a POWER decision the folk advice is right: under a pure location shift the rank test
   gives up a few per cent on normal data and wins outright on every other shape measured.
3. As a HYPOTHESIS decision it is a substitution, and nobody announces the substitution.
4. With unequal spread the rank test misses 5% on 21 of 36 designs
   where its own null is exactly true - in BOTH directions, and on balanced designs too,
   up to 3.6x nominal one way and 14x too quiet the other. Welch is conservative on the
   contaminated cells but by about a fifth, an order of magnitude less.
5. On a 2-point rating scale, dropping the tie correction costs 0.17 of power
   and takes a 5% test down to under 2%. It is not a rounding detail.

The honest report is not one test or the other. It is the mean difference AND P(B > A),
because when they disagree that disagreement is the finding.
