====================================================================================================
2. THE DESIGN CONSTANT: the alarm rate is arithmetic - and 1-(1-0.025)^K is the wrong arithmetic
====================================================================================================
Homogeneous world: every segment has exactly the same true effect, so any segment the scan
calls HARMED is a false alarm. The number everybody quotes for this is 1-(1-0.025)^K = 39.7%
at K=20. That expression is this build's world at a true effect of ZERO. With a real effect
the whole family shifts and the alarm rate falls - so the closed form needs the effect in it:

    P(harm flag) = 1 - (1 - Phi(-1.96 - ate/se_seg))^K,   se_seg = sigma*sqrt(4K/n)

   K  true ate  se_seg  measured  naive cf  this cf    bonf      bh
  20      0.00   0.100     0.406     0.397    0.397   0.029   0.030
  20      0.05   0.100     0.150     0.397    0.130   0.007   0.009
  20      0.10   0.100     0.032     0.397    0.030   0.000   0.001
   5      0.05   0.050     0.009     0.119    0.008   0.001   0.002
  50      0.05   0.158     0.435     0.718    0.437   0.009   0.011

The naive form is out by a factor of 3 at K=20 on a launch that worked (0.397 vs 0.130). The
form with the effect in it matches to MC error everywhere except K=50, where a segment holds
80 users per arm and the normal approximation inside the test is itself liberal.

The uncomfortable reading: a scan is MOST likely to invent a harmed segment exactly when the
launch did nothing at all, and a strong launch is partly self-protecting. The story 'it
worked overall but hurt segment 7' is least trustworthy in the cases people tell it about.

NEGATIVE RESULT - the house pattern does NOT hold here. Days 165/167/168 all found a bias or
a size flat in n while the experiment's power rose. This one MOVES, because the reference is
not a zero effect: ate/se_seg grows with sqrt(n), so a bigger test is a safer scan.
        n  measured  closed form  overall power
    2,000     0.258        0.239          0.210
    8,000     0.118        0.130          0.623
   32,000     0.028        0.030          0.997
  128,000     0.000        0.001          1.000

====================================================================================================
3. The worst segment's number is not an estimate of that segment's effect
====================================================================================================
K=20, n=8,000, true effect +0.05 in EVERY segment. The segment that came out worst:
  reported effect                 -0.1365  +/- 0.0014
  closed form ate + se*E[min Z20] -0.1373   (E[min Z20] = -1.8675)
  same segment, fresh sample      +0.0446  <- the truth is +0.05
  and it is called significant    0.132 of the time

The reported harm is 1.86 standard errors of pure selection. Nothing about that
segment produced it; the MINIMUM operator produced it, and the quadrature knows the number
before the experiment runs (predicted -0.1373, measured -0.1365, gap 0.0008).
Re-running the same segment returns the truth, which is why 'it did not replicate' is the
usual epilogue and why nobody learns anything from it.

With a real harmed segment (tau -0.25 in segment 0) the worst segment IS segment 0
88.2% of the time - the RANKING carries information even where the number does not.

====================================================================================================
4. NEGATIVE RESULT: a segment scan is blind by construction, because a segment is 1/K of the data
====================================================================================================
n=8,000, K=20. The experiment's own MDE is 0.0626; a segment holds 400 users, so
its MDE is 0.2802 - 4.47x larger, exactly sqrt(K), and a Bonferroni-corrected
segment test is larger again. That is arithmetic. The simulation only confirms it.

 harm delta  true seg tau  pre-reg 1  scan 20 bonf  scan 20 bh  overall +ve
       0.10         -0.05      0.078         0.004       0.004        0.514
       0.30         -0.25      0.652         0.266       0.278        0.312
       0.60         -0.55      1.000         0.990       0.990        0.152
       1.00         -0.95      1.000         1.000       1.000        0.030
       2.00         -1.95      1.000         1.000       1.000        0.000

'pre-reg 1' is the same test at alpha 0.05 on ONE named segment: naming the segment in advance
costs nothing in power and deletes section 2 entirely. The gap pre-reg -> bonf is the price of
the other 19 looks - 0.652 to 0.266 at delta 0.30, i.e. scanning costs 59% of the detections.
Also visible: the overall column FALLS as the harm grows, because one segment's harm is diluted
1/K. At delta 1.00 the launch is a wash overall (ATE +0.000) while a twentieth of users lose
0.95 - the aggregate test is not a weak version of the segment question, it is a different one.

How much traffic does the guard need to reach 0.80 on a harm the size of the ATE itself?
         n  P(catch, bonf)  overall power
     8,000           0.007          0.500
    40,000           0.027          1.000
   200,000           0.277          1.000
 1,000,000           1.000          1.000

The experiment is done at 40,000. The guard protecting it is at 0.027 there and needs roughly
25x that traffic to be worth reading - the same shape as Day 165's SRM guard and Day 168's
dose-response guard: calibrated, cheap to run, and blind at the sample size you have.

5. 'We looked at 20 slices' is not 'we ran 20 tests' - and the gap is measurable
====================================================================================================
20 overlapping binary cuts (mobile / new / EU / ...), each about half the users, sharing one
latent factor with loading rho. True effect 0 everywhere, so every flag is a false alarm and
k-effective is read straight off the alarm rate.

  rho  P(harm flag)  k effective  bonf FWER  raw power  bonf power
 0.00         0.226         10.1      0.026      0.738       0.365
 0.30         0.183          8.0      0.011      0.685       0.270
 0.60         0.176          7.6      0.019      0.680       0.278
 0.90         0.137          5.8      0.009      0.690       0.315
 0.99         0.069          2.8      0.004      0.708       0.300

Even at rho=0 the 20 cuts behave like ~10 tests, not 20 - independent DEFINITIONS still make
dependent ESTIMATES, because two half-sample slices share a quarter of the users, giving a
correlation of 0.5 between their effect estimates before any correlation in the cuts
themselves. So Bonferroni over overlapping slices is already 2x conservative at rho=0, and
by rho=0.99 it is 7x: k-effective 2.8, family rate 0.069, alpha divided by 20 anyway, and
power more than halved (0.708 raw -> 0.300). Both numbers are computable from the slice
memberships alone, before any outcome is looked at - and neither is the number 20.

6. NEGATIVE RESULT: the CATE tree's subgroup is a FITTED object, and its effect is its objective
====================================================================================================
True world: tau = 0.05 for EVERY user, five covariates, none of them doing anything. There is
no heterogeneity to find. Fit a depth-2 transformed-outcome tree, then read the worst leaf's
effect two ways - from the data that chose the split, and from a held-out half.

leaves found                       3.62
worst leaf, in-sample effect       -0.2924   truth +0.05
worst leaf, honest effect          +0.0450
in-sample CI covers the truth      0.303   <- nominal 0.95
honest CI covers the truth         0.950
in-sample calls it harmed (p<.05)  0.527
honest calls it harmed (p<.05)     0.007

A subgroup that does not exist is reported as harmed by half of these runs, with an interval
that covers the truth 0.30 of the time. The tree is not misbehaving - it was asked for the
split that maximises the difference in the effect signal, and reading the effect off the same
rows is reading the objective function back out. Day 169 found this in a synthetic control's
pre-period fit; it is the same failure with a different fitted object, and the fix is the same
shape: score the thing on data that did not choose it.

With REAL heterogeneity the honest half is not free - it costs half the rows:
  beta=0.00  leaf |error| in-sample 0.2156 honest 0.1004   coverage 0.597 / 0.948
  beta=0.10  leaf |error| in-sample 0.2081 honest 0.0945   coverage 0.642 / 0.961
  beta=0.20  leaf |error| in-sample 0.1939 honest 0.1037   coverage 0.690 / 0.950
  beta=0.40  leaf |error| in-sample 0.1327 honest 0.0918   coverage 0.857 / 0.949
The in-sample leaf estimate is worse at EVERY beta, including beta=0.40 where the
heterogeneity is loud - selection bias does not wash out once the signal is real, it just
stops being the largest term. Honest coverage sits at nominal across the whole range.

7. NEGATIVE RESULT: a post-assignment segment breaks the parts, and the check that sees it is algebra
====================================================================================================
True effect is +0.05 for everybody; treatment also makes engagement more likely.
  overall effect (correct)            +0.0510
  'engaged users' segment             -0.0944
  'not engaged' segment               -0.1097
  per-segment count check fires       1.000  (SRM test at 0.0005, Day 165)

BOTH parts say the launch hurt while the whole says it helped. That is arithmetically
impossible for a real partition, and it is the tell: conditioning on a variable treatment
moved makes each segment a different population in each arm.

So make it a check. Share-weighted mean of the segment effects, minus the overall effect, in
units of the overall SE - no counterfactual, no assumption, and nobody runs it:
  pre-assignment partition   -0.00012 sd 0.00698   (max |gap| 0.0468)
  post-assignment gate       -8.40145 sd 0.50645
  separation                 1204 legit-sd

This is the only guard in the build without a power problem, and the reason is that it is not
a test: for a partition fixed before assignment the identity holds up to O(1/n), so the
legit distribution has almost no width. Days 165, 168 and 169 all ended with a calibrated
guard that could not see the failure it was built for. The one that works here works because
it checks an accounting identity instead of estimating a parameter.

====================================================================================================
8. What the 'no segment may be harmed' gate actually buys
====================================================================================================
        gate   blocks a launch that helped everyone   catches a real -0.25 segment
         raw                                  0.143                          0.728
  bonferroni                                  0.002                          0.278
          bh                                  0.003                          0.302

Read the two columns together before choosing. The uncorrected gate is the one people run and
it is the only one that ever blocks a good launch. Correcting it makes the false alarms
vanish and leaves detection where section 4 put it - which means the gate is honest about
being weak rather than dishonest about being strong. If a segment genuinely matters, the way
to protect it is to name it before the test and size for it, not to scan for it afterwards.
