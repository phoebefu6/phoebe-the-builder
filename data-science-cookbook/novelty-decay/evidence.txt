2. The design constant: how users were let in changes the reported lift, on its own
====================================================================================================
Attenuation A = the user-day-weighted mean of exp(-t/lam) over the window. The reported
lift is tau_inf + (tau0 - tau_inf) * A. There is no n, no sigma and no outcome in A -
it is a property of the release calendar.

  T/lam   A bigbang   A uniform   ratio  lift bigbang  lift uniform   apart
      1      0.6783      0.7736   1.140        0.0743        0.0819  10.3%
      2      0.4639      0.5988   1.291        0.0571        0.0679  18.9%
      4      0.2634      0.3998   1.518        0.0411        0.0520  26.6%
      8      0.1341      0.2329   1.737        0.0307        0.0386  25.7%
     12      0.0894      0.1630   1.823        0.0272        0.0330  21.7%

    enroll  closed form   measured   +/- mc
   bigbang       0.0411     0.0412   0.0003
   uniform       0.0520     0.0521   0.0004

continuous-time forms at T=4 lam: bigbang (lam/T)(1-e^-T/lam) = 0.2454,
uniform (2lam/T)(1-(lam/T)(1-e^-T/lam)) = 0.3773; the discrete daily sums are 0.2634
and 0.3998, i.e. the integral understates the daily version by ~6.8%.

Two tests of identical length, on identical users, on an identical effect, report lifts
26.6% apart at T = 4 lam. Continuous enrollment keeps the population younger, so it
weights the novelty component UP - the opposite of the intuition that a ramp dilutes it.

    T  T/lam  fit tau_inf       sd  mean CI width  covers 0  covers 5x      r2

   84   12.0       0.0191   0.0056         0.0202     0.092      0.000   0.107

At T = lam the 95% interval on the asymptote is 609 wide on a quantity of 0.02 - it excludes
nothing anybody would decide between, and the point estimate is negative. The interval is
usable from about T = 8 lam, where the estimate is 0.0177 against a truth of 0.0200 with an
sd of 0.0088. THE FIT IS ONLY RELIABLE IN THE REGIME WHERE IT IS UNNECESSARY: at 8 decay
constants the last window of the test has already measured the asymptote directly, so the
extrapolation earns its keep on exactly the windows where nobody needs it.

NEGATIVE RESULT: r2 runs the WRONG WAY down this ladder - 0.314 at the useless T = lam and
0.107 at the reliable T = 12 lam - because a longer window is mostly flat curve, so there is
less variance for the model to explain precisely when the model is trustworthy. A reader
choosing between two fits on goodness of fit picks the shorter, worse one. This is Day 169's
mechanism in a new field: the diagnostic scores the fit on the tenures that were measured,
and the quantity is defined at the tenures that were not.

The guard is calibrated (0.035 on the null) and it is the first one in four builds that is
neither inert nor free: it needs roughly 6x the users to reach 0.61 where the experiment is
already at 1.000, and ~24x to be as sure about the decay as the test is about the effect.
That is a real price on a ladder real tests reach, unlike the SRM check (Day 165), the
dose-response check (Day 168) and the segment scan (Day 170), which all needed traffic
nobody has. It also answers a different question: it detects that the lift MOVED, and says
nothing about where it settles. A guard can be well-powered for the wrong estimand.

5. Decay and a cohort mix shift are the same published plot, five times apart in the decision
====================================================================================================
Constructed, not fitted: solve for cohort effects m_k, CONSTANT in tenure, whose aggregate
lift-by-tenure curve equals the decay curve exactly. Only cohorts enrolled by day T-1-t are
old enough to appear at tenure t, so the tenure-t lift is a prefix mean of m, and pinning
those prefix means inverts in closed form.

cohort effects run 0.0217 to 0.3875; mean(m) = 0.100000 = tau0 exactly (gap 0.00e+00)

NEGATIVE RESULT, and it is a limit on the previous build's best find: the free accounting
guard does NOT generalise here. Day 170's share-weighted identity separated a legitimate
partition from a post-assignment one by 1,204 sd with no threshold at all. The tenure
version closes to machine precision in BOTH worlds, because tenure is a legitimate
partition in both - a mix shift and a decay are each perfectly consistent bookkeeping. An
identity can only catch a violation of itself, and neither failure here is one.

The published plot cannot tell these apart and never will - they are the same curve by
construction. What separates them is a COLUMN, not a test: with the enrollment date in the
table, real decay bends every cohort's own curve and a mix shift bends none of them. Same
shape as Day 168's cluster level - the fix has to live at the level that contains the
mechanism, and no amount of the aggregate can substitute for it.

7. What a long-term holdout costs, and why one holdout cannot attribute two launches
====================================================================================================
The recommended fix is to hold a slice of users out for months and read the gap. Its
resolution is arithmetic - MDE ratio against a 50/50 test is 1/(2 sqrt(p(1-p))), with no n
and no sigma in it.

 holdout share  MDE x 50/50  traffic-time to match
           50%        1.000                   1.0x
           20%        1.250                   1.6x
           10%        1.667                   2.8x
            5%        2.294                   5.3x
            1%        5.025                  25.3x

A 5% holdout carries 2.29x the MDE of the 50/50 test that shipped the feature, and the
quantity it must resolve is SMALLER: tau_inf/tau0 = 0.20 here. Both together, the holdout
needs 132x the user-days of the original test to reach the same power - which is why
the honest version of this fix is measured in quarters, not weeks.

measured: 95/5 split, 84 days, reading tenures 56-84 gives 0.0200 +/- 0.0096 (sd across runs)
against a truth of 0.0200; per-run |z| beats 1.96 in 0.560 of runs at this size

joint cost: 200,000 users, a 5% holdout, 240 days, daily gap SE 0.01026;
a dedicated 50/50 test on 100,000 users would read one launch at SE 0.00447.

              release calendar  SE per launch  SE of total  x dedicated       cond
     6 launches, 30 days apart        0.00246      0.00132         0.6x       84.8
     6 launches, 14 days apart        0.00352      0.00087         0.8x      259.9
      6 launches, 7 days apart        0.00496      0.00078         1.1x      594.8
      6 launches, 3 days apart        0.00757      0.00073         1.7x     1490.1
      6 launches, 1 days apart        0.01310      0.00072         2.9x     4624.9
  6 launches, all the same day   UNIDENTIFIED           --           --        inf

A holdout accumulates every launch shipped past it, so its gap is a JOINT COST and the
RELEASE CALENDAR, not the statistics, decides whether one launch's share is recoverable.
Same structure as Day 161's warehouse invoice: the total is clean at every spacing
(0.00132 to 0.00072) and the split is a design choice.

NEGATIVE RESULT, and it is the opposite of the intuition that a busy calendar is hopeless:
attribution is nearly FREE while the launches are separable, and then falls off a cliff. Six
launches 30 days apart cost 0.55x a dedicated test per launch - BELOW 1, because
240 days of daily gaps on a small holdout carry more information than one readout of a big
test - rising only to 2.93x at 1 day apart. Then, on the same day, the per-launch
effects are not identified at ANY sample size, because the design matrix loses RANK rather
than becoming imprecise. There is no gradient to manage at the end: the calendar either
separates the launches or the question is unanswerable, and which of the two you are in is
computable from the ship dates alone before any data exists. Cross-link to Day 169's
permutation floor - another design constant with no data in it that decides whether an
analysis has any outcome at all.

                  policy  mean claim      bias       sd     RMSE  x truth
      pooled 4-week lift      0.0521    0.0321   0.0061   0.0327    2.61x
         final-week lift      0.0214    0.0014   0.0224   0.0224    1.07x

The bias-variance split is the whole decision and it is not a tie. The two window readouts
are precise and wrong by a factor; the fitted asymptote is both biased and 25x noisier than
the readout it replaces, so its RMSE is 7x worse than the naive pooled lift; the holdout is
that is both centred and useful, and it costs three months. There is no readout of a
four-week test that answers the question, so the choice is which wrong number to ship on -
and the pooled lift, the default, is the one that is confidently wrong.
