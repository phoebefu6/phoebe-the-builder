
================================================================================================
1.  THE GEOMETRY  -  exact, closed form, no simulation
================================================================================================

Two independent estimates, standard errors s1 and s2, intervals at multiplier z:
    bars separate when   |d| > z * (s1 + s2)
    test rejects when    |d| > z_alpha * sqrt(s1^2 + s2^2)
and s1 + s2 >= sqrt(s1^2 + s2^2) always, so NON-OVERLAP IS THE STRICTER EVENT.

At equal standard errors the bars demand the means be sqrt(2) = 1.4142x further apart than the test does.
The interval level at which the two events COINCIDE is 83.4%, not 95%  (z_alpha / sqrt(2) = 1.3859).
At 95% bars and equal standard errors, two bars may still share 58.6% of one arm and the difference is significant  (2 - sqrt(2)).

  sd ratio   R = threshold    matching level   overlap still allowed
             inflation        for p < 0.05     at the 95% boundary
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
    1.00      1.4142             83.4%            58.6% of an arm
    1.25      1.4056             83.7%            57.7% of an arm
    1.50      1.3868             84.2%            55.8% of an arm
    2.00      1.3416             85.6%            50.9% of an arm
    3.00      1.2649             87.9%            41.9% of an arm
    5.00      1.1767             90.4%            30.0% of an arm
   10.00      1.0945             92.7%            17.3% of an arm
   30.00      1.0328             94.2%             6.3% of an arm

READ THIS ROW ORDER TWICE.  The rule is WORST at a sd ratio of 1 - two groups of the same
size and spread, the design everyone trusts - and gets progressively less wrong as the
groups become lopsided.  'A little overlap is fine' has no number: the permitted overlap
runs from 58.6% of an arm down to 6.3% across this table.

Exhaustive check over 4,000 standard-error ratios in [0.01, 50]: the significance
threshold never exceeds the non-overlap threshold (worst ratio 0.990149 <= 1).

================================================================================================
2.  CALIBRATION  -  the theorem, verified before any result is read
================================================================================================

If the bars and the test share their standard errors, 'bars apart' must IMPLY
'significant', with no exceptions. Anything else means the harness is broken.

  design            replicates   gap AND not significant    overlap | significant
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   30/30  sd 1.0/1.0     200,000           0.000000             0.3846
  100/20  sd 1.0/1.0     200,000           0.000000             0.2575
  100/5   sd 1.0/1.0     200,000           0.000000             0.1811
  200/6   sd 1.0/2.5     200,000           0.000000             0.0776
   40/8   sd 1.0/1.0     200,000           0.000000             0.3200

  0 violations in 1,000,000 replicates, as the algebra requires.

Now break it on purpose. Keep the SAME bars - drawn from a design-time standard error, the
way a chart made from a power calculation or a published SD is - and test with the observed
variance instead (Welch). The two objects no longer share an SE and the guarantee is gone:

  design            gap AND not significant   verdict
  --------------------------------------------------------------
   30/30  sd 1.0/1.0             0.00000   -
  100/20  sd 1.0/1.0             0.00151   REVERSED
  100/5   sd 1.0/1.0             0.15554   REVERSED
  200/6   sd 1.0/2.5             0.17827   REVERSED
   40/8   sd 1.0/1.0             0.02737   REVERSED

  Worst: 200/6 at 0.1783 - on that
  design nearly one reading in five shows a VISIBLE GAP between bars that the test will not call
  significant. The rule's one guarantee survives exactly as long as the picture and the
  test are computed from the same number.

3.  THE DEAD ZONE  -  measured: significant results the picture tells you to discard
================================================================================================

95% bars, Welch t-test at alpha = 0.05, normal data, 40,000 replicate studies a design.

  --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  P(overlap | significant) spans 0.0739 to 0.5236 across these designs. On the 30/30 design at 64% power,
  52.4% of the studies that found a real, significant difference
  would be thrown away by a reader applying the overlap rule to the very same chart.

  Negative result: the dead zone SHRINKS with power, because a large true effect pushes the
  bars past each other too. It is not a bug that only appears in bad studies - it is worst
  at exactly the effect sizes that are marginal enough to be worth arguing about.

================================================================================================
3b.  THE RULE IS A 0.6% TEST WEARING A 5% LABEL  -  exact, then measured
================================================================================================

Read 'the bars do not touch' as a decision rule and it has a size like any other test.
Under a true null the difference is N(0, sqrt(s1^2 + s2^2)) and the rule fires at
z_level * R standard errors, so its size is 2 * (1 - Phi(z_level * R)) - no simulation.

  At equal standard errors and 95% bars that is 0.0056.
  A reader applying the overlap rule to a 95% chart is running a 0.6% test, and reporting
  it as a 5% one.

   sd ratio   exact size of the rule   measured P(gap) under a true null   99% Wilson
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      1.0                  0.0056                              0.0059   [0.0050, 0.0070]
      2.0                  0.0085                              0.0083   [0.0072, 0.0095]
      3.0                  0.0132                              0.0124   [0.0111, 0.0139]
      6.0                  0.0241                              0.0229   [0.0211, 0.0249]

  The Welch test on those same replicates holds its nominal rate throughout:
    sd ratio  1.0   Welch 0.0505  ok
    sd ratio  2.0   Welch 0.0500  ok
    sd ratio  3.0   Welch 0.0493  ok
    sd ratio  6.0   Welch 0.0487  ok
  so the gap between the two columns is the RULE's and not the data's. The rule's size
  climbs toward 5% only as the standard errors diverge - the same direction as everything
  else here, for the same reason: R falls from sqrt(2) toward 1.

================================================================================================
4.  THE FIX IS A LEVEL, NOT A CORRECTION  -  measured
================================================================================================

Draw the bars at 83.4% instead of 95% and, for equal
standard errors, 'they do not touch' and 'p < 0.05' become the same event. Measured as the
rate at which the two verdicts DISAGREE on the same replicate:

   n1/n2   sd1/sd2   disagreement at 95%   at the matching level   matching level
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   30/30    1.0/1.0               0.3287                  0.0031           83.4%
   30/30    1.0/1.0               0.2741                  0.0019           83.4%
   30/30    1.0/1.0               0.1331                  0.0009           83.4%
   60/60    1.0/1.0               0.3211                  0.0013           83.4%
   60/60    1.0/1.0               0.2270                  0.0007           83.4%
  100/100   1.0/1.0               0.2925                  0.0008           83.4%
   30/30    1.0/3.0               0.1926                  0.0155           87.9%
   30/30    1.0/6.0               0.1065                  0.0121           91.1%

  The residual at the matching level is not zero because the bars use each group's own t
  multiplier while Welch uses a pooled-ish df - the algebra is exact for z, approximate for
  t. It is an order of magnitude smaller than the 95% disagreement on every row.

================================================================================================
5.  THE REVERSAL  -  correlated measurements, where the rule fails the OTHER way
================================================================================================

Everything above says the rule is too STRICT. Pair the measurements and it becomes too
LOOSE: the test knows the correlation, the bars cannot show it, and the same picture now
hides a real difference behind two bars sitting on top of each other.

n = 30 pairs, true difference 0.35, 95% bars per arm, 40,000 replicates.

   rho    slack (x further the bars demand)   P(sig)   P(overlap | sig)   99% CI
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   0.00                          1.414   0.2561           0.7215   [0.7099, 0.7328]
   0.30                          1.690   0.3488           0.8778   [0.8705, 0.8848]
   0.50                          2.000   0.4558           0.9460   [0.9416, 0.9502]
   0.70                          2.582   0.6659           0.9879   [0.9861, 0.9895]
   0.85                          3.651   0.9215           0.9984   [0.9977, 0.9988]
   0.95                          6.325   1.0000           0.9999   [0.9996, 1.0000]

  At rho = 0.95, the test rejects on 1.0000 of studies and 0.9999 of those
  rejections have overlapping bars. The independent-groups algebra caps the rule's
  conservatism at sqrt(2) = 1.414x; correlation removes the cap (6.32x here) and it grows without bound as rho approaches 1.
  Nothing on the chart distinguishes this case from the independent one.

  A Wald interval on x = 0 has ZERO WIDTH. It is a dot on the axis, so it cannot overlap
  anything, and the rule reports a difference the test refuses to call. On the second block
  the failure rate sits just under P(x1 = 0) - the mechanism is not subtle once
  named, and it is invisible on the chart, where a zero-width bar looks like a precise one.
  How closely: the three Wald rows sit 0.0393, 0.0014 and 0.0071 below P(x1 in {0, n1}).
  The first block is the loose one - at p1 = 0.001 the other arm is small enough that some
  zero-count outcomes clear the test anyway, so the two quantities are close without being
  the same event, and this build states the gap rather than claiming an equality.
  Wilson, Agresti-Coull and Clopper-Pearson never reverse on any design here, because none
  of them can produce a degenerate interval.

================================================================================================
WHAT TO TAKE AWAY
================================================================================================

1. EXACT. At equal standard errors the bars demand 1.414x the separation the test does. The
   level at which the two agree is 83.4%, and two 95% bars
   may share 58.6% of an arm and still be
   significant. Both numbers are properties of the DESIGN, not of the picture.
2. MEASURED. Up to 52.4% of genuinely significant results have
   overlapping 95% bars, and the share is largest where the argument is closest.
3. MEASURED. On paired data at rho = 0.95 the rule fails the other way: 0.9999 of
   real rejections sit behind overlapping bars, and the chart cannot show rho.
4. EXACT. Wald coverage bottoms out at 0.3318 against a nominal 0.95, and
   a Wald bar on zero events has zero width, which reverses the rule's one guarantee.
5. The fix is to draw the bars at the matching level and say so in the caption, or to stop
   drawing two intervals and draw the interval for the DIFFERENCE, which is the estimate
   the question was about in the first place.
