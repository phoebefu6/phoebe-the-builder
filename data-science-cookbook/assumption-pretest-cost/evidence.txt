====================================================================================================
ASSUMPTION-PRETEST COST: what 'we checked the variances first' actually does
====================================================================================================

Both groups always have the SAME mean, so the null is exactly true everywhere and
every rejection printed is a false positive. A correct 0.05 procedure reads 0.05.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. THE HARNESS MEASURING ITSELF
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Student's t on a balanced, equal-variance, normal design, 20,000 reps, 10 seeds.
  This cell is EXACT - its true error rate is 0.0500 with no approximation anywhere.
  Observed spread: 0.0476 to 0.0517   (half-width 0.0020)
  99% Wilson interval on a true 0.05 at 20,000 reps: [0.0462, 0.0541], width 0.0079
  The band is 0.010 wide, so this replicate count can resolve it.
  Nothing below is called broken on a point estimate.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   0.05); the rest are power. All at alpha = 0.05.

  design                           levene   brown-forsythe         bartlett           f-test
  n=20/20 sd=1:1                    0.058            0.044            0.051            0.051
  n=50/10 sd=1:1                    0.051            0.043            0.047            0.048
  n=50/10 sd=1:1.5                  0.354            0.297            0.364            0.408
  n=50/10 sd=1:2                    0.737            0.681            0.771            0.801
  n=50/10 sd=1:3                    0.969            0.956            0.977            0.981

   Read the two sd=1:1 rows first: that is each pretest's own false-positive rate,
   and a pretest that cannot hit 0.05 on equal variances has no business gating
   anything. Then read down the power columns.

   The same four on NON-NORMAL data with EQUAL variances - still all false positives:

  population                       levene   brown-forsythe         bartlett           f-test
  normal                            0.059            0.042            0.053            0.053
  t5                                0.054            0.038            0.155            0.155
  lognormal                         0.111            0.046            0.226            0.226
  uniform                           0.057            0.034            0.004            0.004

   Bartlett and the F-test are not variance tests on real data - they are normality
   detectors wearing a variance test's name. Their rejections on the heavy-tailed and
   skewed rows are reporting a shape difference that is not there, on groups whose
   variances are IDENTICAL. Brown-Forsythe is the one that holds.

3. THE MECHANISM: THE GATE HANDS STUDENT'S ITS WORST CASES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The pretest reads the sample variances. Student's pooled standard error reads the
   same sample variances. So 'the pretest passed' does not select a random subset of
   samples - it selects the ones whose variance ratio happened to look small.

  design                  share passed  Student overall   Student | PASSED   ratio
  n=20/20 sd=1:3                 0.025           0.0544             0.0909   1.67x
  n=50/10 sd=1:1.5               0.697           0.1361             0.1406   1.03x
  n=50/10 sd=1:2                 0.324           0.2064             0.2352   1.14x
  n=50/10 sd=1:3                 0.046           0.2873             0.3717   1.29x
  n=30/10 sd=1:1.5               0.754           0.1067             0.1109   1.04x
  n=30/10 sd=1:2                 0.402           0.1509             0.1747   1.16x
  n=10/50 sd=1:3                 0.056           0.0010             0.0018   1.80x
  n=10/30 sd=1:3                 0.112           0.0037             0.0045   1.21x

   Every ratio above 1.00 is the procedure working backwards: on the samples the
   pretest cleared, Student's is MORE wrong than it is on average, not less. The gate
   is not filtering out the dangerous cases - it is concentrating them, because a
   sample where the small group's variance came out low both passes the pretest and
   understates the pooled standard error.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   n=50/10, walking the variance ratio. Watch the gated column rise and then fall.

   sd ratio  pretest fires   student     welch     GATED   gated / nominal
       1.00          0.045    0.0470    0.0488    0.0491    0.98x
       1.25          0.117    0.0893    0.0490    0.0809    1.62x
       1.50          0.298    0.1318    0.0483    0.0987    1.97x
       2.00          0.679    0.2039    0.0486    0.0906    1.81x
       2.50          0.877    0.2556    0.0486    0.0671    1.34x
       3.00          0.954    0.2922    0.0482    0.0566    1.13x
       4.00          0.993    0.3350    0.0475    0.0491    0.98x
       6.00          1.000    0.3745    0.0471    0.0471    0.94x

   Worst at sd ratio 1.5: the gated procedure runs at 0.0987, 2.0x nominal,
   while Welch on the identical samples holds 0.0483.

   The shape is the point. At a huge gap the pretest always fires, the procedure
   collapses into Welch, and it is fine. At no gap there is nothing to get wrong. The
   damage is in the middle - which is where the pretest returns p > 0.05 and the
   analyst writes down 'variances were checked and found equal'.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DOES A LIBERAL PRETEST ALPHA FIX IT?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   The standard advice for a low-power pretest is to run it at a generous alpha so it
   errs toward rejecting. Measured on the worst design found above:

   pretest alpha    fires   GATED error   vs Welch
            0.01    0.145        0.1145   +0.0655
            0.05    0.298        0.0998   +0.0508
            0.10    0.401        0.0900   +0.0410
            0.20    0.528        0.0777   +0.0288
            0.50    0.751        0.0604   +0.0115
            1.00    1.000        0.0490   +0.0000

   alpha = 1.00 is the pretest always rejecting, i.e. unconditional Welch - it is in
   the table as the known-answer anchor, and it lands on Welch's own rate. Raising
   the pretest alpha does help, monotonically. What it converges to is the procedure
   that skips the pretest entirely, which is the cheapest possible way to get there.

6. THE OTHER HALF: WHAT EACH PROCEDURE DETECTS WHEN THERE IS SOMETHING TO DETECT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   A procedure that never rejects is not safe, it is deaf. Same designs, now with a
   real difference in means. Higher is better here.

  design                   shift   student     welch     gated   comparable?
  n=50/10 sd=1:3            1.50    0.6915    0.2914    0.3004   NO - Student's runs at 0.2873 under the null, so its lead here is that same inflation
  n=10/50 sd=1:3            1.50    0.2273    0.7889    0.7647   YES, and it is the finding - Student's runs at 0.0010 under the null, so this deficit is real
  n=20/20 sd=1:1            0.80    0.6933    0.6922    0.6932   yes - both hold 0.05 under the null
  n=50/10 sd=1:1.5          1.00    0.6876    0.4515    0.5754   NO - Student's runs at 0.1361 under the null, so its lead here is that same inflation

   Read the last column before the numbers. Where Student's is INFLATED under the
   null, its apparent lead here is that same inflation - it rejects more often
   because it rejects more often, not because it detects more. Those rows are not a
   power comparison at all.

   The n=10/50 row is the opposite case and the one nobody audits. There Student's
   holds 0.2273 detection against Welch's 0.7889 - a real deficit,
   honestly paid for, because its false-positive rate under the null is BELOW
   nominal. A Type I audit would have called that design safe and said nothing
   about the detections it was quietly giving up.
