1. A 95% band is a claim about a frequency - kept, and at what width
====================================================================================================
  nominal 95%, h=1..12, 600 independent futures, well-specified model, iid noise

  method                        coverage   at h=1  at h=12  mean width  vs narrowest
  gaussian sqrt(h)                 0.994    0.945    1.000       38.13         2.71x
  gaussian flat                    0.942    0.945    0.937       15.65         1.11x
  gaussian + parameter term        0.948    0.948    0.943       15.94         1.13x
  empirical residual               0.932    0.933    0.928       15.06         1.07x
  rolling h-step quantile          0.897    0.908    0.888       14.07         1.00x
  split conformal                  0.967    0.980    0.968       18.90         1.34x
  bootstrap path                   0.932    0.937    0.927       15.25         1.08x

  The band almost every forecast ships - residual sd widened by sqrt(h) - does not
  undercover here. It OVERCOVERS, and pays for it in a width nobody can act on.

2. The sqrt(h) fan is an assumption about the MODEL, and it is measurable before shipping
====================================================================================================
  trend+season fit         sd(h=1) 3.777  sd(h=12) 3.938  ratio 1.04  fitted exponent +0.010
  random walk + naive      sd(h=1) 3.715  sd(h=12) 13.640  ratio 3.67  fitted exponent +0.519

  sqrt(12) = 3.46. The random walk grows at exactly that rate (exponent 0.5) because its
  h-step error is a SUM of h independent shocks. A fitted trend+season forecast makes a
  DIRECT h-step statement whose error does not accumulate at all - the fan is borrowed
  from a different estimator. Two lines of numpy tell you which world you are in.

3. The parameter-uncertainty term: what dropping it costs, and where
====================================================================================================
  1500 futures per row, so a coverage figure here carries a Monte Carlo se of about 0.0056

   train points   leverage at h=12   flat band  + param term  width cost
             36             0.3079       0.896         0.937       1.12x
             60             0.1435       0.928         0.945       1.06x
            120             0.0597       0.949         0.959       1.03x
            240             0.0273       0.950         0.957       1.01x
            480             0.0131       0.953         0.955       1.01x

  The term costs almost nothing to include and is the difference between a band that
  keeps its promise at 36 training points and one that does not. It is also the term
  that grows as you forecast further from the training window - the honest version of
  the fan the previous section just rejected.

4. Conformal's guarantee is MARGINAL, and marginal is not the promise people hear
====================================================================================================
  a two-regime world: quiet blocks and volatile blocks, 3x apart in sd, average variance
  held EQUAL to the single-regime world, so nothing in the aggregate looks different -
  verified: mean variance 15.99 vs 16.00 in the flat world
  (the obvious construction, sigma*sqrt(r) and sigma/sqrt(r), is 4.6x louder on average and
  would have made this a test of MORE noise rather than of the SAME noise, unevenly spread)

  method                        marginal  in quiet  in volatile   spread
  gaussian + parameter term        0.926     1.000        0.850    0.150
  split conformal                  0.950     1.000        0.898    0.102
  rolling h-step quantile          0.855     0.997        0.708    0.289

  NEGATIVE RESULT: the marginal number is the one everybody reports and it is kept. The
  user standing in the volatile regime - the only regime where an interval matters - gets
  a materially different frequency, and no marginal check in existence can see it,
  because the two errors are constructed to cancel in the average.

5. Pointwise coverage is not path coverage, and the horizons are not independent tests
====================================================================================================
  pointwise coverage                    0.948
  whole-path coverage (all 12 inside)    0.535   <- the number a planner needs
  if the horizons were independent      0.525

  A 95% band is kept 95% of the time AT A POINT and contains the whole twelve-step path
  barely half the time. Those are different claims and only one of them is published.

  How much widening a simultaneous band needs depends on how many INDEPENDENT horizon
  tests the path really is - k_eff = log(path coverage) / log(pointwise coverage):

     rho   pointwise     path    k_eff of the 12 horizons
     0.0       0.951    0.563                       11.38
     0.4       0.945    0.550                       10.54
     0.7       0.931    0.593                        7.27
     0.9       0.891    0.625                        4.08

  At rho=0 the twelve horizons behave like ~12 independent tests, so the Bonferroni
  intuition is right; by rho=0.9 the path is a handful of tests and Bonferroni is
  wasteful. The correction is measurable rather than assumed.


  NEGATIVE RESULT - small per-point shortfalls COMPOUND along the path. Raise the
  per-point level to 99% and measured per-point coverage is 0.9876, short of nominal
  by 0.0024 - a rounding error at a point. Over twelve horizons it becomes
  0.861 against the 0.886 the nominal level promises (measured path 0.865),
  i.e. a 0.2-point miss at the point is a 2-point miss on the path. Part of the shortfall
  is z-for-t: a band 2.58 estimated sds wide is more sensitive to the sd being estimated
  than one 1.96 wide, and the t quantile recovers some of it, but only some:
    z quantile (2.576) at nominal 99%: 0.9876  (short by 0.0024)
    t quantile (2.606) at nominal 99%: 0.9886  (short by 0.0014, 41% of the gap closed)
  The rest is the estimated sd itself, and it does not go away by choosing a nicer
  quantile - it goes away by measuring coverage in the tail you intend to quote.

6. Autocorrelated noise: the same interval, swept over rho
====================================================================================================
     rho   gaussian + param   split conformal   rolling quantile  true sd(h=12)/sd(h=1)
     0.0              0.953             0.972              0.896                   0.94
     0.4              0.941             0.962              0.874                   0.98
     0.7              0.926             0.945              0.816                   1.03
     0.9              0.883             0.899              0.679                   1.12

  The residual sd a fitted model reports is the sd of a CORRELATED series, and at rho=0.9
  the in-sample residuals understate what the next twelve points will do. Note which
  method degrades least, and that none of them is repaired by widening with sqrt(h).

7. The check itself: how much data it takes to catch a band that is 5 points short
====================================================================================================
  power of a one-sided binomial check that a nominally 95% band really covers 90%,
  at alpha=0.05, as a function of the test points available:

    test points m    if independent    at k_eff=1/12    at k_eff=1/20
               20             0.326            0.164            0.152
               50             0.493            0.196            0.176
              100             0.681            0.238            0.205
              250             0.925            0.332            0.272
              500             0.994            0.453            0.359
             1000             1.000            0.628            0.493
             5000             1.000            0.986            0.925

  and measured: a coverage figure computed from 20 overlapping rolling windows has sd
  0.048 across repeats - so 0.95 and 0.90 are within one standard
  deviation of each other on the evidence a normal backtest supplies.

  DESIGN CONSTANT, no data in it: catching a 5-point coverage shortfall needs hundreds of
  INDEPENDENT test points, and Day 172 measured that 20 rolling windows stepped by 1 are
  worth about one. A published 'we validated the intervals' is usually a check with no
  power, which is why bad bands survive.

8. The scoreboard: coverage can always be bought, a proper score cannot
====================================================================================================
  method                        coverage    width  interval score  rank by score
  gaussian sqrt(h)                 0.996    37.95           38.22              7
  gaussian flat                    0.942    15.57           18.90              2
  gaussian + parameter term        0.946    15.87           18.87              1
  empirical residual               0.932    15.00           19.03              3
  rolling h-step quantile          0.881    13.89           21.74              6
  split conformal                  0.966    18.64           20.53              5
  bootstrap path                   0.933    15.20           19.11              4

  best by interval score: gaussian + parameter term
  best by coverage alone: gaussian + parameter term
  Coverage is not a score: a band can buy it by getting wider, which is exactly what the
  sqrt(h) fan does. The interval score charges for width and for misses in the same units,
  so it is the column to rank on - and it disagrees with the coverage column.
